Bryo

FM-1 firmware · GPL-3.0

Firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1

Moss grows on tape.

Bryo turns the FM-1 into a four-track instrument for growing sounds. Play a loop, record over it, and let it drift. Everything is a pad and a knob away.

/ 01

4 Track Sound Sculpting Instrument

Bryo is new firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1. It gives you four tracks. Each one starts with a sound and sends it through four effects, always in the same order.

Let's start with the tape, because everything grows from it. A tape is a loop that lives in memory. Press PLAY and it plays. Press a white key and you hear one of its sixteen slices. Press REC and it records the other three tracks onto itself, so you can bounce an idea down and keep building on top.

The four tracks share 28 seconds of memory. A track only takes what it records, so three short loops leave room for one long one.

There are no menus. Each page shows four values, and the four knobs change those four values. Press a pad again to see its next page. Hold a pad and the white keys light up, one for each page, so you can jump straight there.

Every screen on this site comes from the same code that runs on the FM-1. What you see here is what you'll see on the device.

/ 02

One line, five stages

A sound always takes the same path: the source, then GRAIN, RESONATOR, COLOR and SPACE. Since the order never changes, you always know where to look. Each effect's first page ends with its mix knobs. Press EDIT or FX, turn KNOB 4, and you'll hear the effect fade in.

Source Home

Pick a tape loop, a small synth, a sampler that spreads one sound across the keys, or a drum machine. Hold HOME and turn SELECT to switch. Each source remembers its own knobs, so you can switch back without losing anything.

The first time you turn it on, the four tracks play four factory reels: BEAT, BELL, DRON and FOLD.

Grain Edit

GRAIN listens to the last few bars of the track and plays them back as tiny overlapping pieces. You can stretch them out, hold them on one spot, or let them trail behind with SCAN.

Hold the black 0 key to freeze what it heard into a loop. Tap it instead and the freeze stays on until you tap again. BODY adds a low floor under the grains.

Resonator Edit

Four tuned strings that the track's sound rings through. Their notes come from the project's key and scale, so they always agree with each other.

On this page the white keys set the root note and pluck the strings. The strings also have an arpeggiator of their own, RES ARP.

Color FX

Drive, bit crush, noise that follows the sound, and a tilt EQ. The page draws a sine wave through the whole stage, so you can see what the distortion does to it before you hear it.

Space FX

A delay and a small room. All four tracks share them, and each track decides how much to send with its own DLY and VERB knobs.

They only use memory while you're using them, so a dry project has more room for tape.

/ 03

Four ways to make a sound

Tape

A loop in memory with sixteen slices on the keys. Move where it starts and ends, change its speed, or flip it backwards.

Synth

A small subtractive synth with up to three voices, a filter and its own envelope.

Poly

Plays any reel, or the track's own tape, across the keys. Up to four notes at once, each with its own envelope and filter.

Drum

Sixteen drums, all synthesized, inspired by the CR-78. Ten patterns to start from, and SEED plays them with small changes, the way a drummer would.

Hold a pad. Turn a knob.

Each track has four modulators. They move knobs for you, so a sound keeps changing while your hands are busy.

Three of them live on the LFO pad, and each can be an LFO, an ADSR envelope or an envelope follower. The fourth, on SEQ, is a step sequencer.

To make a knob move, hold LFO or SEQ and turn that knob. How far you turn sets how far it moves, from −100 to 100 %. A knob that's moving gets a purple mark, so you can see what's alive at a glance.

/ 04

Hold a chord, hear a pattern

Hold a few keys and the arpeggiator plays them one at a time, in time with the tempo.

Every track has one, and it does something a little different on each source. On SYNTH and POLY it plays notes. On TAPE it steps through the slices you hold. On DRUM it walks through the drums you hold: hold kick, snare and hat with UPDN and you get kick, snare, hat, snare, kick.

Mode
KEY plays while you hold the keys. LTCH keeps the last chord going after you let go. ADD keeps every key you press, so a pattern grows one note at a time.
Order
Up, down, up and down, random, or the order you pressed them.
Range
One to three octaves (for notes; a slice or a drum has no octave).
Rate
Quarter notes down to 32nds, with triplets.
Page 2
GATE for short or long notes, SWNG to push every other step late.

To clear a latched or piled-up pattern, hold the black POLY key on the ARP page.

A second one, for the strings

Press ARP a third time and you're on RES ARP. It plucks RESONATOR's four strings, one note per step, whatever the track itself is playing. Turn RESONATOR's WET up to hear it. A latched pattern keeps plucking after you leave the page, so you can set it going and go shape something else.

Both follow the transport. STOP quiets them and keeps the notes, and PLAY starts them again from the top. Saving a project keeps the notes too.

/ 05

Specs

Hardware
M-VAVE FM-1 (JieLi AC79), bare metal
Tracks
4, each with a source and the full chain
Sources
TAPE, SYNTH, POLY, DRUM
Effects
GRAIN, RESONATOR, COLOR, SPACE (the delay and room are shared sends)
Modulation
4 per track: 3 that can each be an LFO, ADSR or FOLLOW, and a step sequencer
Arpeggiators
1 per track (notes, slices or drums), plus RES ARP for RESONATOR's strings
Tape memory
28 s shared across the tracks, IMA ADPCM at 22.05 kHz
Mixer
Level, LOW, HIGH, FILT and PAN per track; a master compressor; the project's key and scale
Storage
6 projects, 8 track presets plus 8 factory ones, 4 factory reels plus 6 of yours
USB
Shows up as a drive called BRYO (WAV in and out), and records what the computer plays, up to 23.6 s
Status
Phases 1 to 9 of 10 built; running on an FM-1 since 2026-10-10
Licence
GPL-3.0-only, same as Felucca
/ 06

Get it

There's no release yet. Bryo runs on a real FM-1 now, and I'd like it to be played for a while before anyone trusts it with their music.

If you're happy building firmware, you can try it today. Bryo keeps Felucca's update path exactly as it was, so you can always go back.

/ 07

Thank you, Felucca

Bryo exists because Felucca exists.

Leo Kuroshita (@kurogedelic) and Hügelton Instruments did the hard part. They worked out how the FM-1 works, wrote firmware for it from scratch, and shipped thirteen engines, a 4-track sequencer, USB audio and a web installer anyone can use. Then they shared all of it under the GPL, which is how I got to learn from it and build on it.

Bryo started from Felucca 1.0.3 (commit b22a24b), copied in unchanged so every change since is easy to see. It still runs on Felucca's hardware layer, update loader, USB and storage, with Leo's copyright lines in place. If Bryo is useful to you, please thank them too.
